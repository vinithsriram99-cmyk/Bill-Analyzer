THE AGRICULTURAL PRODUCE MARKETING (REFORM) BILL, 2026
(Introduced by the Minister of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare)
Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

Farmers in many states can sell their crops only in government regulated mandis, where they often face long queues, middlemen and delayed payments. The Essential Commodities Act, 1955 limits the storage of agricultural produce, which discourages investment in warehouses and cold storage. This Bill seeks to give farmers the freedom to sell their produce to any buyer, to improve payment security, to protect small farmers from unfair contracts and to increase investment in agriculture.

A BILL to reform the marketing of agricultural produce and to provide for matters connected therewith.

1. Short title. This Act may be called the Agricultural Produce Marketing (Reform) Act, 2026.

2. Freedom to sell. A farmer may sell agricultural produce, including crops, vegetables, fruits and livestock products, inside or outside the mandi to any buyer of his choice.

3. Payment security. A buyer shall pay the farmer within three working days. A farmer shall not be required to pay any market fee or cess on sales made outside the mandi.

4. Contract farming. A farmer may enter into a written contract with a buyer before sowing of a crop. The contract shall fix the price of the produce and shall not transfer the ownership of the land of the farmer.

5. Minimum support price. The Central Government shall continue to announce the minimum support price for the main crops every year, and purchase of crops under the minimum support price shall continue.

6. Dispute resolution. A farmer may bring a dispute with a buyer to the Sub-Divisional Magistrate, who shall decide the matter within thirty days. Any buyer who fails to pay a farmer shall be liable to a penalty.

7. Storage and warehouses. The limits on storage of agricultural produce under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955 shall not apply to warehouses and cold storage, except in case of a steep rise in prices.

8. Rules. The Central Government may, by notification, make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act.
