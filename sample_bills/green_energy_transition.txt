THE GREEN ENERGY TRANSITION BILL, 2026
(Introduced by the Minister of New and Renewable Energy)
Ministry of New and Renewable Energy

STATEMENT OF OBJECTS AND REASONS

India has committed to reduce carbon emissions and to increase the share of renewable energy in its electricity supply. Coal based power plants still produce most of the electricity and they cause heavy air pollution and climate damage. Solar and wind energy projects face delays in land acquisition, grid connection and clearances from several departments. This Bill seeks to speed up the transition to clean energy by creating a single window for approvals and by setting yearly targets for renewable energy. It also provides support to workers in coal regions so that they can move to new jobs in the clean energy sector.

A BILL to promote renewable energy, to reduce carbon emissions and to provide for matters connected therewith.

1. Short title. This Act may be called the Green Energy Transition Act, 2026. It extends to the whole of India.

2. Renewable energy targets. The Central Government shall set a yearly target for solar, wind and other renewable energy capacity. Every state government shall prepare a plan to meet its share of the target.

3. Single window clearance. A National Renewable Energy Authority shall be established to give all approvals for renewable energy projects within ninety days. Projects of solar parks and wind farms shall be given priority for grid connection.

4. Carbon emission limits. Every thermal power plant shall measure its carbon emission and report it every year. A power plant that exceeds the emission limit shall pay a levy as may be prescribed. The levy shall be used to fund clean energy projects and forest protection.

5. Rooftop solar. Every new government building and every large commercial building shall install rooftop solar panels. Households that install solar panels may sell extra energy to the electricity grid.

6. Support for workers. The Central Government shall set up a fund for training and re-skilling of workers in coal mining regions. Workers who lose jobs due to the closure of coal plants shall be given priority in new energy projects.

7. Environment protection. No renewable energy project shall be built in a protected forest, wildlife sanctuary or wetland without clearance from the environment authority.

8. Waste management. Solar panels and batteries that are no longer usable shall be collected and recycled by their producers. The producers shall register with the Authority and submit a yearly return on waste collected.

9. Rules. The Central Government may, by notification, make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act.
